Leonardo DRS

Jun – Aug 2026
Program management intern · Danbury, CT

Leonardo DRS is a defense technology company. In program management, the question was always the same: is this program on track? I pulled its five key numbers into one view and built price estimates from past jobs.

  • Built a prototype dashboard bringing five focus areas into one view, plus top-level financial models that cut prep time
  • Helped build two pricing-estimate tools from historical data
  • Found inconsistent data in a data warehouse
  • Used AI to turn repeat work into tools
  1. 1Five numbers, one view. Revenue so far, revenue expected, cash coming in, people on the program and work booked. One screen instead of five reports.
  2. 2Cash this week and next, so there are no surprises.
  3. 3Actual vs plan, month by month, so a program running behind shows up early.
  4. 4Price estimates from history. Enter the hours and materials and get a likely range, based on similar past jobs.

Trumpf

Jan – May 2026
Digital intern · Farmington, CT

Trumpf makes industrial lasers and sheet-metal machines, and trains its customers to run them at its Farmington training center. I worked on the customer side: are companies using the training seats they bought, would pay-per-class customers be better off subscribing, and who's up for renewal?

  • Built a data model behind about $300K in tracked funds
  • Found and fixed inconsistent data moving between Excel, cloud systems and other software
  • Built three Power BI reports for business decisions
  1. 1The program at a glance. Subscriptions, how many seats get used, renewals coming up and pay-per-class customers.
  2. 2Seat use by customer. Low use means a company isn't getting value from what it bought, so it's a follow-up before renewal.
  3. 3Pay-per-class vs subscription. When a company keeps paying class by class and spends more than a subscription costs, it's time to offer one.
  4. 4Renewals and follow-ups. Who expires soon and who to call, so nothing lapses quietly.
